Part 1 — What your condo is worth right now

I completely understand the frustration. You bought about eight years ago in the low $600s, and on paper it can feel like you're not gaining anything. But here's the part that matters most: you're not just selling — you're buying again, into the same market. Whatever has softened on the sale side has also softened on the buy side. So the gap between what you sell for and what you pay next is likely to stay the same or even work in your favour, which means your mortgage position shouldn't get worse, and could get better. You're trading sideways in a market that's discounted on both ends — and that's not a bad time to move.

Now to your unit specifically. It's a renovated, ~899 sq ft unit backing onto the ravine, and both of those — the renovations and the ravine view — add real value. The question is how much, and where the ceiling is. Here's what the comparable units tell us:

Part 2 — How we get you the best result: the offer-day strategy

This is where the strategy earns its keep. My recommendation is to list at $598,000, hold offers for at least 12 days, then review all offers on one set offer date. Here's why that works in your favour right now:

  • Listing at $598K — below that $649,900 comparable — makes your unit the obvious value play and pulls in the most showings, fast.
  • The 12-day window builds a pool of motivated buyers instead of us fielding offers one at a time.
  • Reviewing everything on a single offer date puts those buyers in competition with each other. That's the engine that drives the final price up into the mid-$600s and gives us a real shot at the optimistic end.
  • We also have a built-in advantage: two units are actively for sale right now at $649,900 and $719,000. Any buyer shopping this building sees those prices first — which makes your unit, with its ravine view and renovations, look like the smart buy. We use their list prices to our benefit.

The sold prices are what they are — we can't change those — but we can absolutely control how we position your unit against the two active listings, and that's the lever we pull.
